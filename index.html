Quantum nanoscience · School of Integrated Circuits

Nanoelectronics and Quantum Transport Laboratory (NQTL)

Huazhong University of Science and Technology · Wuhan National High Magnetic Field Center · Wuhan, China

We study how electrons move through atomically thin semiconductors, and build the devices that follow: 16-nm-channel WSe2 transistors approaching the ballistic limit, annealing-free ferroelectric FETs, and oxide thin-film devices that hold off more than a kilovolt. The group covers the whole chain — material growth, nanofabrication, and electrical characterization down to cryogenic temperatures.

Research

2D semiconductor transistors

CVD-grown monolayer WSe2 and WS2, channels scaled to 16 nm, and p-type contacts engineered to reach record on-current at a ballistic efficiency of 81%.

Ferroelectric and oxide-semiconductor devices

Annealing-free wurtzite ferroelectric FETs and ALD InGaO transistors for neuromorphic computing and capacitorless DRAM, with defect engineering for long retention.

High-voltage oxide thin-film devices

BEOL-compatible InGaO TFTs with breakdown voltages above 1.3 kV, and top-gate architectures that improve the on-resistance versus breakdown trade-off.
