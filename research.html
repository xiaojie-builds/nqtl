Emerging New Materials

Emerging Materials focuses on discovering and engineering functional materials for future electronics. We study high-k dielectrics, high-thermal-conductivity films, 2D semiconductors, oxide semiconductors, and nitride ferroelectrics, with emphasis on low-temperature synthesis and BEOL compatibility. Our work connects atomic structure, defects, and interfaces with electrical, thermal, and ferroelectric properties.

By developing scalable growth and integration strategies, we aim to build new material platforms for logic, memory, power, and heterogeneous systems. This direction provides the material foundation for next-generation semiconductor technologies that are faster, more energy efficient, and more highly integrated.

Quantum Transport and Device Physics

Quantum Transport and Device Physics explores charge transport and emergent phenomena in low-dimensional devices under extreme conditions, including cryogenic temperature, high magnetic field, and strong electrostatic control. We investigate ballistic transport, quantum oscillations, quantum Hall states, and interaction-driven effects in high-quality semiconductor and van der Waals systems.

Our goal is to understand how interfaces, contacts, disorder, and confinement shape carrier dynamics and quantum coherence. Through advanced device fabrication and precise low-temperature measurements, we reveal new physical mechanisms and enable quantum-enabled electronics. This direction links fundamental condensed-matter physics with next-generation devices for cryogenic circuits and quantum information technologies.

Advanced Electronics

Advanced Electronics develops high-performance, low-power semiconductor devices for future logic, memory, and power applications. We explore ultrathin channels, advanced gate stacks, low-resistance contacts, capacitorless DRAM, ferroelectric memory, and wide-bandgap power transistors.

Our research addresses key challenges such as short-channel effects, leakage, variability, switching speed, retention, endurance, and high-field reliability. By combining new materials with innovative device architectures and interface engineering, we aim to improve energy efficiency and functionality at highly scaled dimensions. This direction creates device technologies for beyond-CMOS computing, high-density memory, and efficient power conversion.

Advanced Integration and Computing

Advanced Integration and Computing focuses on monolithic 3D integration, heterogeneous system integration, and neuromorphic computing hardware. We vertically combine logic, memory, sensing, and emerging devices to reduce interconnect delay, increase bandwidth, and improve system-level energy efficiency. At the same time, we develop brain-inspired devices and circuits that integrate storage and computation for intelligent sensing and in-memory processing.

Our work emphasizes BEOL-compatible fabrication, thermal management, interconnect engineering, and scalable array design. This direction aims to build compact, multifunctional, and energy-efficient hardware platforms for future electronics, edge intelligence, and artificial intelligence systems.
